# 74HC595 shift register

**Not yet confirmed** against the physical part — but unlike the breakouts in
this folder, this is a standard 16-pin DIP with a fixed, industry-wide pinout.
What you do need to check is **which end is pin 1**: the notch (or the dimple)
marks it, and pins count anticlockwise from there.

```
        ┌──∪──┐
  Q1 ●1 │     │ 16● VCC
  Q2 ●2 │     │ 15● Q0
  Q3 ●3 │     │ 14● DS     (serial data in)
  Q4 ●4 │     │ 13● OE     (output enable, active LOW -> tie to GND)
  Q5 ●5 │     │ 12● ST_CP  (latch / "storage clock")
  Q6 ●6 │     │ 11● SH_CP  (shift clock)
  Q7 ●7 │     │ 10● MR     (master reset, active LOW -> tie to VCC)
 GND ●8 │     │  9● Q7'    (serial out, to the next chip's DS)
        └─────┘
```

**`OE` to GND and `MR` to VCC are not optional.** Left floating, the outputs
either stay dark or reset at random, and it looks like a wiring fault.

## What it buys you

Three GPIOs in, eight outputs out. Chain them by running `Q7'` into the next
chip's `DS` — a second chip costs **zero** extra GPIOs, just one more byte
shifted out before you latch.

That is how ten bar-graph segments fit on a board with three pins to spare.

## How it is driven

Shift all the bits out on `DS`/`SH_CP`, then pulse `ST_CP` once. The outputs
change only on that pulse, which is the point: they never show the intermediate
states while the bits are moving through.

## Voltage

- Powered at 3.3 V, an ESP32 drives it comfortably in spec.
- Powered at 5 V, a 74**HC**595 wants 3.5 V to read a HIGH — a 3.3 V ESP32 is
  marginally under. It usually works; a 74**HCT**595 is the part that is
  specified to.
