How to read it

The extension board owns the middle

The board straddles the channel at positions 13–32, with its pins in columns d and g. Its silkscreen labels are drawn in place, so you can find any GPIO by eye.

  • Only three rows are reachable per side. The body physically covers d–g, so e and f are live but blocked. Everything taps from a/b/c below or h/i/j above — that constraint is what drives the routing lanes.
  • The two halves never cross. Every port signal and the buzzer are on the d side; all four LEDs are on the g side. Nothing has to jump the channel.
  • Positions 1–12 aren't wasted. The buzzer stage lives there, on the far side of the extension board, so its jumper from GPIO 12 doesn't collide with the three port runs.
  • Rails do the power distribution. 5V and GND leave the extension board once each, into the bottom rails, and everything else taps locally.
Clear the board first

Your breadboard currently has the 74HC595, the LED bar graph, an accelerometer breakout and the coin-cell rig on it from positions 34 down. These layouts assume 33–63 is empty.

Still worth checking

One derived fact

The silkscreen labels were read straight off your photo. The position numbers were derived by lining the breadboard's printed ruler up against the label rows — it agrees in four places (15↔36/VP, 20↔33, 25↔12, 30↔3.3V), but it's still read off a photograph.

Before you commit wire to board, confirm one pin: GPIO 13 should sit two positions below GPIO 12 on the left-hand labels. If that holds, the whole map holds. Full details in docs/extension-board.md.
